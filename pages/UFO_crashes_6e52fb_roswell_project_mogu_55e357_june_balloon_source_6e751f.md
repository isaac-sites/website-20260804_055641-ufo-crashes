## Introduction

The most specific version of the Project Mogul explanation for the [Roswell]({{ 'roswell/' | relative_url }}) debris is not simply that it came from “a weather balloon”, but that it probably originated from a particular lost balloon train launched in early June 1947. The candidate most often identified is the New York University balloon array commonly called **[Flight 4]({{ 'flight-4/' | relative_url }})**, launched from Alamogordo and tracked towards the Corona area before contact was lost. The case is significant because it offers a concrete explanation that matches the timing, location and unusual construction of the debris. At the same time, it remains a probability rather than a certainty. No surviving serial number, recovered instrument or contemporaneous document conclusively links the Foster ranch debris to that specific launch. The question is therefore not whether the identification is possible, but how strong the evidence actually is.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://muller.lbl.gov/teaching/physics10/Roswell/USMogulReport.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: muller.lbl.gov">[Muller Lab]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">muller.lbl.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Muller Lab Project Mogul</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Muller Lab Project Mogul</span></span></span>

## What Albert Crary's diary records

One of the most important primary sources is the field diary kept by geophysicist **Albert P. Crary**, who directed the scientific operations associated with Project Mogul's early balloon work.

The diary entry for **4 June 1947** has become central to the debate because it records that planned balloon operations were disrupted by cloud cover. Crary noted that there was "no balloon flight" because of the weather, but he also described flying a regular sonobuoy using a cluster of balloons with successful reception on the ground. This wording has generated two competing interpretations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://muller.lbl.gov/teaching/physics10/Roswell/USMogulReport.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: muller.lbl.gov">[Muller Lab]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">muller.lbl.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Muller Lab Project Mogul</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Muller Lab Project Mogul</span></span></span>

Supporters of the Mogul explanation argue that the diary distinguishes between an intended full experimental mission and the simpler balloon cluster that was actually launched. In this reading, the balloon array later designated as Flight 4 was nevertheless released, even if the day's programme differed from the original plan.

Critics argue that the same diary indicates that no formal Mogul flight occurred on 4 June, making the later Air Force identification internally inconsistent. They point out that the official numbering of these early launches was not always recorded contemporaneously, allowing room for disagreement over whether the June launch should properly be called "Flight 4" at all.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://muller.lbl.gov/teaching/physics10/Roswell/USMogulReport.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: muller.lbl.gov">[Muller Lab]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">muller.lbl.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Muller Lab Project Mogul</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Muller Lab Project Mogul</span></span></span>

The diary therefore supports the existence of a balloon launch on that date, but it does not by itself prove that the recovered Roswell debris came from it.

## Why the projected route points towards Corona

The strongest circumstantial evidence concerns the balloon's projected path.

Tracking records reconstructed from the launch indicate that the balloon cluster travelled east-north-east from Alamogordo towards the Corona region of New Mexico. According to later reconstructions by Project Mogul participants, the tracking stations followed the balloon until equipment failures caused it to disappear from observation while it was still approaching the area where W. W. "Mac" Brazel later discovered scattered debris. Estimates place the last known position within roughly 15 to 20 miles of the Foster ranch.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://skepticalinquirer.org/2017/12/the-roswell-incident-at-70-facts-not-myths/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: skepticalinquirer.org">[Skeptical Inquirer]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">skepticalinquirer.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on skepticalinquirer.org.</span></span></span>

This geographical coincidence matters because the debris field described in July 1947 consisted largely of lightweight materials spread across a broad area rather than heavy aircraft wreckage. A long train of balloons, radar reflectors, cords and lightweight instrument components breaking apart after descending into open ranch country would be expected to produce exactly this kind of dispersed field.

Equally important is the chronology. Brazel reported finding the material well before the publicity surrounding the "flying saucer" announcement in July. The Air Force later argued that this timing excludes the later polyethylene balloon flights conducted after 28 June and instead points towards one of the early June neoprene balloon launches.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://muller.lbl.gov/teaching/physics10/Roswell/USMogulReport.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: muller.lbl.gov">[Muller Lab]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">muller.lbl.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Muller Lab Project Mogul</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Muller Lab Project Mogul</span></span></span>

## Why Flight 4 remains probable but unproven

The identification of the Roswell debris with Flight 4 rests on several independent pieces of evidence rather than a single decisive document.

Among the strongest points are:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* the existence of a June balloon launch that was not recovered;
* a projected flight path towards the Corona area;
* debris descriptions involving foil, rubber, sticks, cord and radar reflector components consistent with Mogul equipment;
* the lack of contemporary reports describing engines, heavy structural parts or other aircraft components.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://muller.lbl.gov/teaching/physics10/Roswell/USMogulReport.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: muller.lbl.gov">[lbl.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">muller.lbl.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Muller Lab Project Mogul</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Muller Lab Project Mogul</span></span></span>

</div>

However, important limitations remain.

No surviving inventory identifies the recovered debris by serial number. No preserved fragment can be matched scientifically to Project [Mogul hardware]({{ 'mogul-hardware/' | relative_url }}). The surviving field documentation from 1947 is incomplete, and some relevant military records were later destroyed or lost, preventing definitive reconstruction. Furthermore, because the early Mogul launches were experimental, documentation of flight numbering and configurations is less complete than for later operations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://muller.lbl.gov/teaching/physics10/Roswell/USMogulReport.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: muller.lbl.gov">[Muller Lab]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">muller.lbl.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Muller Lab Project Mogul</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Muller Lab Project Mogul</span></span></span>

Historians therefore distinguish between **consistency** and **proof**. The evidence consistently points towards a missing early June balloon train, but it falls short of demonstrating that this particular launch unquestionably landed on the Foster ranch.

## How later investigations assessed the evidence

The U.S. Air Force's 1994 investigation concluded that the recovered Roswell material was, with a high degree of confidence, the remains of Project Mogul Flight 4. That assessment relied on the combined fit between witness descriptions, Crary's diary, launch records, photographs of the displayed debris and the known characteristics of Mogul balloon trains rather than on any single conclusive artefact.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://muller.lbl.gov/teaching/physics10/Roswell/USMogulReport.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: muller.lbl.gov">[Muller Lab]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">muller.lbl.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Muller Lab Project Mogul</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Muller Lab Project Mogul</span></span></span>

Independent researchers have reached varying conclusions. Some, including former Project Mogul personnel and sceptical investigators, regard the Flight 4 identification as the best available historical explanation because it explains both the unusual materials and the military's confused handling of a classified project. Others argue that ambiguities in the launch records and diary entries prevent the case from being considered settled.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://skepticalinquirer.org/2017/12/the-roswell-incident-at-70-facts-not-myths/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: skepticalinquirer.org">[Skeptical Inquirer]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">skepticalinquirer.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on skepticalinquirer.org.</span></span></span>

For that reason, the missing June balloon train is best understood as the **leading historical hypothesis** within the Project Mogul explanation rather than a conclusively demonstrated fact. Its strength lies in the convergence of timing, [trajectory]({{ 'trajectory/' | relative_url }}) and debris characteristics, while its principal weakness is the absence of a surviving piece of evidence that directly links the Foster ranch material to that specific launch.

      if (!languageMarket && localeMarketMap[languageOnly]) languageMarket = localeMarketMap[languageOnly];

    var matches = localeMatches();

    for (var i = 0; i< timezoneRules.length; i += 1) {
      var rule = timezoneRules[i] || {};
      try {
        if (new RegExp(rule.pattern).test(tz)) return rule.market;
      } catch (err) {}
    }
    return '';
  }

    if (localeMarkets.language && available.indexOf(localeMarkets.language) >= 0) return localeMarkets.language;
